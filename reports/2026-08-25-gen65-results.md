# Gen65 dust-completion and mint-insurance results

## Verdict

Gen65 did not prove profitability, but it produced the first direct paper
example of the inventory mechanic seen in the clean paired-bid winner: earlier
cheap complete pairs subsidized a marginal late complement and converted a
large one-sided settlement tail into an almost flat book. That is materially
closer to the observed winner than a directional signal or repeated minting.

The $0.95 mint repair cannot be called simply good or bad. It paid away neutral
edge in both executions. Once it saved the arm from a much larger settlement
loss; once waiting was better. It is insurance, and two claims are not enough
to price it.

## The winner-like dust execution

In the 17:10 window the baseline ended with 20.00 Up and 15.02 Down shares.
Its cash was -$17.3092, giving +$2.6908 if Up won but -$2.2892 if Down won.
At T+270 the dust twin bought the five-share market minimum of Down at 0.08,
including $0.02576 of taker fee. It ended at 20.00 Up and 20.02 Down:

- Up settlement: +$2.26504
- Down settlement: +$2.28504
- unmatched inventory: 0.02 share
- completed-pair average: about $0.8867

The marginal pair itself cost slightly above $1 fee-inclusive, but earlier
cheap pairs kept the rolling basket far below the $0.99 cap. That is the exact
economic principle present in the clean winner history: judge marginal
completion against the whole inventory book, not as an isolated bet. This does
not prove that winner uses our T+270 timing or taker repair.

## The mint repair is insurance

There were two real $0.95 repair executions:

1. At 17:10 it locked a $0.22487 loss. The maker control and $1.00 arm waited
   another 65.4 seconds and completed for +$0.15. Repair cost roughly $0.375.
2. At 17:15 it locked a $0.18624 loss. The control and $1.00 arm never sold the
   Down complement; Up won, so their unmatched position lost $2.30. Repair
   saved roughly $2.114.

This is why selecting only on pair spread is wrong. The $0.95 floor reduces
tail exposure by purchasing certainty, while $1.00 preserves neutral edge and
accepts binary settlement risk. Gen66 retains both; the decision variable is
the empirical insurance premium versus avoided tail loss over many due
episodes, not whether the first or second anecdote won.

## Signal and latency

The causal official-TWAP signs available in Gen65 were -0.64, +3.77, and +3.02
bps, and all three matched settlement direction. Across Gen61-65 this is 11/16
overall, 8/11 for `abs(signal) >= 1 bp`, and 5/6 for the post-hoc
`abs(signal) >= 2 bp` subset. That is encouraging but still small,
regime-sensitive, and selected on the same short run. The signal remains
shadow-only.

Normal public feed age remained about 9-12 ms median and local ordered-queue
residence stayed at or below 8 ms. Source-age tails reached 1.508 seconds. At
17:20:20 the public socket closed with code 1013, `slow consumer: send buffer
full`, despite low local residence; the active 17:20 window was correctly
invalidated. One second is still not baseline latency. It is an intermittent
upstream/socket tail that must freeze decisions. Authenticated POST/cancel
latency remains unmeasured.

## Gen66 decision

1. Keep the basket baseline, T+270 control, and bounded dust twin unchanged.
2. Keep five-set mint plus both $0.95 and $1.00 repair floors until the
   insurance trade-off has more than two events.
3. Do not connect the TWAP signal to quotes yet.
4. Run the fixed cohort from a clean boundary and verify that dust settlement
   persists without taking down the receiver.
5. Real-money execution remains NO-GO.
