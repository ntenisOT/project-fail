# Generation 69 results — planned refresh rejected

## Decision

**NO-GO for live money. Remove the planned boundary reconnect.**

Generation 69 falsified the connection-age hypothesis. Four planned socket
refreshes completed in 77-92 ms, but the server still closed the replacement
connections with code 1013 (`slow consumer: send buffer full`) 45 seconds and
130 seconds after two of those refreshes. A five-minute socket lifetime was not
the cause, and retaining a forced reconnect would add risk and latency without
fixing the failure.

The run also produced one genuine mint-repair comparison: immediately crossing
at a 0.95 proceeds floor locked a loss and materially underperformed both the
1.00-floor arm and its maker control. This is one episode, not a final policy
estimate, but it is direct evidence against the aggressive repair arm.

No live executor or mintbot ran, and no orders were placed.

### Repair episode at 18:40

All mint arms first sold five Up at $0.58. Their second-side behavior diverged:

- `mintrepair5p95` crossed five Down at $0.39. After taker fees its paired
  proceeds were about $0.953 per set, locking roughly -$0.23.
- `mintrepair5p100` waited five seconds and crossed Down at $0.44. Its paired
  proceeds were about $1.003, approximately flat after fees.
- `mintcycle5` stayed maker and completed Down at $0.45 through two partial
  fills, locking +$0.15 including the modeled maker rebate.

The 0.95-floor arm therefore destroyed about $0.38 relative to the maker
control in this episode. A repair rule prevents directional exposure by paying
for immediacy; it does not create edge. The evidence now says the current p95
price is too aggressive.

At 18:45 all three mint arms completed naturally at a $1.03 gross sell pair,
so no repair was due.

## Transport result

Planned refresh-to-subscription times were:

- 18:45:00.047 to 18:45:00.124: 77 ms
- 18:50:00.041 to 18:50:00.133: 92 ms
- 18:55:00.042 to 18:55:00.122: 80 ms

Unexpected 1013 closes still occurred at 18:50:45.426 and 18:57:10.644. The
first was only 45 seconds after a planned refresh; the second was 130 seconds
after one. The affected 18:50 strategy-windows were marked invalid at official
resolution. The 18:55 window was also invalidated and was unresolved at stop.

The application-side pipeline was not saturated: its queue high-water was
89/8192, maximum observed residence was 6 ms, parsing remained below 4.3 ms,
and the strategy loop was normally about 1 ms. Normal source-event age was
about 9-14 ms, while the lifetime source tail reached 4.074 seconds. Measured
simulated activation was about 70 ms. A one-second baseline remains false.

The remaining unmeasured buffer is the WebSocket client's internal frame queue.
The installed client pauses transport reads when that finite queue crosses its
configured high-water mark; it is currently 1024 frames. That mechanism could
fill the server's send buffer while our downstream application queue still
looks healthy, but this is a hypothesis, not a proven cause.

## Signal

The 18:45 official-TWAP shadow was +0.20 bp and the outcome was Up. The invalid
18:50 window was +0.31 bp and resolved Down. Both are far below the exploratory
one- and two-basis-point thresholds. No signal was used for orders and this run
adds no evidence for a production strength cutoff.

## Next bounded experiment

1. Remove the failed planned-reconnect behavior and restore safe in-place
   add-before-remove subscription rotation.
2. Instrument the WebSocket client's internal frame depth and raise its finite
   high-water mark from 1024 to 4096. Do not make it unbounded; retain the 400 ms
   fail-closed freshness rule and existing application-queue telemetry.
3. Run the unchanged strategy board for at least three complete windows. A
   successful result requires no 1013 and observed internal depth well below
   4096; otherwise the hypothesis is rejected or the instrumentation identifies
   actual saturation.
4. Only after transport is usable, replace the ineffective 50-share arm with
   the <=0.1-share balanced-tolerance replenishment experiment.

Corrected winner forensics still supports repeated paired maker accumulation
as one real regime. It does not establish universal direct minting, a reliable
trend signal, or anything close to leader scale.
