# Generation 68 results — repeated inventory and transport failure

## Decision

**NO-GO for live money. Do not promote the 50-share arm or a signal.**

Generation 68 produced two useful findings:

1. Raising the sequential basket arm's inventory cap from 20 to 50 shares did
   not change a single fill. The binding constraint was a fractional unmatched
   lot, not capital capacity.
2. The persistent market WebSocket was closed twice with server code 1013 at a
   roughly 9.5-minute interval despite a shallow, fast local pipeline. Feed
   continuity is now the first blocker to another strategy comparison.

The runner was stopped cleanly after two full valid windows resolved. No live
executor or mintbot ran, and no orders were placed.

## Why the 50-share experiment did nothing

`basket99x50` was identical to the 20-share baseline in all eight valid fill
records and all reported mechanics. Neither arm approached 20 shares per side.
Merely raising `max_inventory` therefore did not test winner-scale repetition.

The 18:20 ledger identifies the actual bottleneck. After completing five
pair-shares, partial public prints left a 0.04-share Down remainder. `PairLots`
correctly retains any positive unmatched lot, and `PairWindow` consequently
quotes only the opposite completion side instead of starting another two-sided
cycle. Because the venue minimum order is five shares, tiny fractional residue
can stall replenishment even when inventory is economically balanced.

The next strategy experiment should compare the unchanged baseline with one
arm that treats at most 0.1 unmatched share as *balanced enough to start another
two-sided clip*, while retaining the fractional lot in accounting and enforcing
the same rolling $0.99 pair-cost cap and 20-share inventory cap. This is not a
write-off of dust and must not alter settlement. It is a bounded state-machine
experiment that directly tests repetition without repeating the failed large
clip or independent-ladder exposure.

## WebSocket failure

The market socket received server code 1013 (`slow consumer: send buffer full`)
at 18:16:38 and again at 18:26:04, 9 minutes 25.9 seconds apart. The first
event invalidated the 18:15 strategy window; the second invalidated the active
18:25 window, which remained unresolved when the generation stopped.

The local evidence contradicts an application slow-consumer explanation:

- Before the first close: application queue high-water 63/8192, maximum queue
  residence 2 ms, JSON parse maximum 0.927 ms, loop interval maximum 2 ms.
- Before the second close: application queue high-water 38/8192, maximum queue
  residence 2 ms, JSON parse maximum 0.317 ms, loop interval maximum 2 ms.
- Normal server-event age remained about 8-16 ms at p50. Measured simulated
  action activation was 71 ms. One second is not the baseline.

The repeated connection lifetime suggests a bounded transport A/B rather than
more strategy tuning. The proposed next generation should intentionally replace
the market connection at the five-minute boundary, before any new-window order
can activate, and distinguish that planned zero-exposure refresh from an
unexpected reconnect. Acceptance requires prompt first books, zero missed
new-window exposure, and no mid-window 1013. This deliberately revisits the
persistent-socket choice using new evidence; it should not be described as a
free latency improvement.

## Signal

The valid T+30 official-TWAP signs were +1.63 bp and +0.73 bp; both official
outcomes were Up. The interrupted 18:15 window had -0.10 bp and resolved Down.
All three signs matched their eventual outcomes, moving the accumulated record
from 12/21 to 15/24 overall. At an absolute one-basis-point cutoff it is 9/13;
the apparently stronger >=2 bp subset remains 5/6 and was post-hoc selected.
This does not establish a tradable threshold. The signal remains shadow-only.

## Proposed sequence — not executed

1. **Transport-only generation:** planned boundary refresh versus the current
   persistent socket, with strategy behavior unchanged. Do not score any
   window whose unexpected connection loss occurs after exposure.
2. **Replenishment generation:** if transport is clean, replace the ineffective
   50-share arm with one <=0.1-share balanced-tolerance arm. Keep five-share
   clips, the $0.99 rolling cap, and the 20-share inventory limit.
3. **Mint:** keep `mintcycle5` as the falsification control and retain the two
   repair arms until more repair-due episodes occur. Two clean $1.03 cycles do
   not erase prior one-leg losses; do not increase minted sets.
4. **Signal:** continue causal shadow collection. No quote asymmetry or minimum
   strength gate until an untouched time-ordered cohort has a positive lower
   confidence bound after sacrificed pair opportunities.

The corrected winner evidence still supports repeated two-token maker
accumulation as one real regime. It does not support a universal direct-mint
strategy, and Gen68 does not make us close to the leader's thousands of paired
shares per market.
