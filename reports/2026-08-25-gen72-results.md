# Generation 72 results — timing A/B inconclusive, transport failure reproduced

## Decision

**NO-GO for live money. Do not promote the 240-second completion timer. Pause
strategy tuning until the intermittent market-WebSocket disconnect is isolated
or an independently reliable data path is available.**

Generation 72 produced only two valid resolved windows. The 240-second and
270-second basket arms were identical because the fee-inclusive $0.99 rolling
pair cap did not permit a late completion in either window. A third full window
was invalidated by a `1013 slow consumer: send buffer full` reconnect before
either timer could establish a valid comparison. The timer hypothesis is
therefore untested, not rejected and not supported.

No live executor or mintbot ran, and no order was placed. The paper runner is
stopped and the remote `paper/KILL` sentinel remains present.

## Transport and latency

Two unexpected 1013 closures occurred:

- 20:11:14 UTC, about 74 seconds into the 20:10 window;
- 20:16:11 UTC, about 71 seconds into the 20:15 window.

The reconnect hypothesis based on client saturation is contradicted by direct
telemetry:

- WebSocket internal-frame high-water: 87/1024;
- application queue high-water: 126/8192;
- maximum application residence: 9 ms, 4 ms before the second window opened;
- strategy loop normally about 1 ms, lifetime maximum 34 ms;
- parse maximum 4.666 ms after more than 600,000 frames;
- ordinary source age about 9-14 ms; lifetime source tail 3.145 seconds.

The client never approached its finite frame limit. Raising the queue to 4096
in Generation 70 and restoring 1024 in Generations 71-72 did not explain the
intermittent closes. Planned boundary reconnects also failed in Generation 69.
The honest remaining possibilities are provider-side shedding, another
unmeasured network/OS buffer, or a protocol/client interaction not represented
by the measured queues. It is not defensible to call this fixed.

The blanket one-second latency assumption remains false. Healthy public-feed
age is about 10 ms and modeled action activation was 67-72 ms here. The 0.4-3.1
second events are sparse safety-tail observations, not normal latency.
Authenticated order POST/cancel latency is still unmeasured.

## Signal

The causal official-TWAP shadows were +1.07 bp (outcome Down), +1.78 bp
(outcome Up), and +0.96 bp (outcome Up): two of three signs correct. The first
mint sale was the eventual winning token in the first two observations, making
both fills toxic despite opposite signal outcomes. The signal did not influence
orders and remains too small and adaptively sampled for a production threshold.

## Fresh six-hour winner refresh

A V2-normalized ClickHouse refresh over all 72 resolved BTC windows from
14:05-20:00 UTC reinforces that there is no single universal winner strategy:

- `0x0cb...`: +$4,831 on $135,477, traded both tokens in 94.4% of windows,
  43.4% maker, buy-pair proxy $0.970, sell-pair proxy $1.090, and large average
  net imbalance. This is mixed inventory plus direction, not a pure market maker.
- `0x3048...`: +$1,408 on $50,511, bought both tokens in 100% of 72 windows,
  never sold, buy-pair proxy $0.978, but only 20.9% maker and $1,276 modeled
  taker fees. This is the clearest current pair accumulator.
- `0xdf4...`: +$959 on $20,344, bought both in 84.7% of 72 windows, never sold,
  buy-pair proxy $0.960, 70.9% maker. This is closer to maker-led paired
  acquisition.
- `0x338...` and `0xcd4...`: 100% maker, buy-pair proxies about $0.982, and
  direct merges of about 1,425 and 1,309 sets respectively. They buy pairs and
  recycle capital by merging; no direct splits were observed at those trading
  addresses.
- `0x7cf...`: 99.8% maker, bought both in 87.5% of windows, buy-pair proxy
  $1.016 and sold both in 31.9% at a $1.031 sell-pair proxy. This is a genuine
  mixed maker-flow candidate, but aggregation does not prove ordered mint/sell
  cycles or its source of edge.

The refresh artifact is `out/gen72-top-setters-btc-6h.json`. Pair sums are
matched-share aggregates including modeled taker fees, not sequential cycle
proof; direct CTF values cover only the queried trading address. The robust
commonality is participation on both outcomes. The profitable mechanism varies
between cheap paired buying, merging, mixed two-sided flow, and directional
residual. Replicating a surface signature is not enough.

## Next decision for independent review

The external reviewers should challenge, rather than merely tune, these options:

1. Pause strategy generations and isolate the 1013 with a separate minimal
   receiver, alternative official client/library, network packet/OS-buffer
   telemetry, and a same-token independent endpoint if available.
2. Keep `basket99t270d` as the current best inventory-control candidate, but do
   not infer profitability from six earlier lag-exposed windows or change its
   timer from this invalid A/B.
3. Demote mint to a falsification/control role. A +$0.15 completed cycle cannot
   absorb recurring roughly -$2.25 to -$3.05 incomplete tails without an
   independently demonstrated completion probability above break-even.
4. Deepen per-wallet lifecycle forensics on the fresh pair accumulators and
   merger wallets: exact order timing, replenishment, marginal pair costs,
   inventory trajectories, completion delays, and outcome-conditioned residue.
5. Treat the TWAP sign as observational until a pre-registered, time-ordered
   untouched cohort produces a positive lower confidence bound on net economic
   improvement after missed opportunities, fees, and serial dependence.
