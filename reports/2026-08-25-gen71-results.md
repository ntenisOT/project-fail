# Generation 71 results — dust tolerance rejected

## Decision

**NO-GO for live money. Retire `basket99dust10`; continue the bounded timed-
completion line with a 240-second versus 270-second A/B.**

Generation 71 showed that treating a <=0.1-share remainder as balanced did not
restart profitable complete-pair churn. In the only window where it diverged,
the arm acquired extra unmatched hedge inventory without increasing paired
shares, paid more for the same paired quantity, and barely improved its adverse
floor. The state transition works as designed; the economic hypothesis failed.

The late taker-completion arm again materially reduced residual risk while
respecting the fee-inclusive $0.99 pair cap. The next useful question is timing:
whether starting the same bounded completion at 240 seconds captures more safe
pairs than waiting until 270 seconds, and what edge it gives up to do so.

No live executor or mintbot ran, and no orders were placed.

### Dust arm

Baseline and dust were identical in the first two windows. The third window
created the intended <=0.1-share state and made them diverge:

- Both ended with 5.02 paired shares.
- Baseline paired cost was $4.8680 and unmatched inventory was 4.98 shares.
- Dust paired cost was $4.9682 and unmatched inventory was 3.42 shares.
- Dust therefore did not create another complete pair. It paid $0.1002 more for
  the same paired quantity and acquired 1.56 extra one-sided shares.

Across the cohort that reduced unmatched inventory by 1.6 shares, but neutral
PnL fell from +$1.65 to +$0.96 and adverse floor improved only from -$5.83 to
-$5.74. Its one-second and five-second markouts were also worse than baseline.
The implementation correctly preserved the residual and rolling cap; the
economics do not justify keeping the arm.

### Timed completion

`basket99t270d` executed one bounded completion in the 19:40 window. Baseline
paired only 0.02 shares and ended 4.98 unmatched; timed completion reached five
paired shares at an average $0.871 fee-inclusive cost and ended only 0.02
unmatched. It correctly refused completion in the other two windows when the
$0.99 cap was unavailable.

Across all three windows, timed completion improved neutral PnL by $2.50 and
adverse floor by $4.98 versus baseline while reducing unmatched inventory from
15 to 10 shares. Its realized PnL was still -$0.85 because the remaining
inventory settled adversely. This is risk control with positive paired
economics, not evidence of overall profitability.

Combining Gen70 and Gen71, the 270-second arm has repeatedly improved neutral
and adverse-floor economics relative to baseline. Six windows remain too few
for production, but the effect is now mechanically consistent enough to tune
one parameter rather than invent another strategy.

### Mint

The maker control completed all three five-set cycles at $1.03 gross pair
proceeds, modeled +$0.15 per window. Completion delays were 6.7, 40.3, and
143.7 seconds.

p100 repair was due only in the 143.7-second episode. It completed at 125.9
seconds and net pair proceeds of $1.0084, earning +$0.0419 instead of the maker
control's +$0.15. It paid about $0.108 to remove five-set directional exposure
17.8 seconds earlier. That is a real insurance trade-off, not free profit. Keep
it as a control; do not increase mint size or claim the repair is optimal.

## Transport and latency

- 15/15 accepted strategy-windows were valid; the five invalid records were the
  deliberately excluded startup window and had zero fills.
- Zero reconnects over about 24 minutes with the original 1024-frame limit.
- Internal-frame high-water: 86/1024; application queue high-water: 87/8192;
  local residence maximum: 4 ms; telemetry unavailable: zero.
- Normal source-event age was commonly about 9-15 ms. Measured simulated action
  activation aggregated around 75-85 ms; one lag-exposed basket window reached
  124 ms. One second is not the baseline.
- Source-event tails reached 2.293 seconds and 2,934 stale causal events were
  rejected. This remains an upstream quality risk; larger client buffers and
  forced reconnects did not explain or fix it.

Two consecutive clean generations do not prove the intermittent 1013 problem
is solved. The telemetry does prove client-frame saturation was not its cause.

## Signal

The causal official-TWAP shadows were +3.95 bp (Up), -1.41 bp (Down), and +0.80
bp (Up); all three signs matched the official outcomes. The two observations
above one basis point were also correct. This is interesting but still an
adaptive, tiny sample; the >=2 bp threshold was selected post hoc and has no
untouched validation cohort. No signal influenced orders.

## Next bounded experiment

1. Remove `basket99dust10` and retain the unchanged baseline.
2. Keep `basket99t270d` and add exactly one `basket99t240d` arm with the same
   five-share minimum-aware completion, $0.99 fee-inclusive rolling cap, 0.1
   dust rounding, and 20-share inventory cap.
3. Keep `mintcycle5` and `mintrepair5p100` unchanged. Their role is to measure
   natural completion versus the current insurance cost, not to scale minting.
4. Run at least three complete windows and compare paired shares, pair cost,
   neutral PnL, adverse floor, unmatched inventory, and completion delay.
5. Continue the signal only in shadow. Do not set a quote-asymmetry threshold
   until an untouched, time-ordered cohort is defined in advance and passes a
   lower-confidence-bound test.

We are improving the right mechanic—fee-aware completion of two-sided maker
inventory—but remain far from winner scale and nowhere near live readiness.
