# Gen67 transport-attribution results

## Verdict

Gen67 localized the latency problem. Multi-second market-event tails occurred
while JSON parsing, event-loop scheduling, and the application queue remained
single-digit milliseconds. The ordinary path is fast; the tails arrive stale
from the feed/socket path and must remain fail-closed. No 1013 occurred during
this 20-minute generation, so the telemetry disproves local saturation during
observed tails but does not prove the intermittent close is eliminated.

Strategically, mint completed both five-set cycles for +$0.30. Basket99 bought
cheap pairs but residual direction converted +$0.58 neutral economics into a
-$3.02 realized result. All three basket arms were identical, confirming that
the plain T+270 arm is redundant with its bounded-dust superset.

## Transport attribution

At the final heartbeat the runner had consumed about 589,000 WebSocket frames.
Observed limits over the generation were:

- frame batch: at most two events;
- JSON parse peak: 4.349 ms;
- event-loop lag: about 1 ms median, 7 ms maximum after startup; the 32 ms
  lifetime value occurred during startup;
- application queue residence: at most 5 ms;
- application queue high-water: 72 of 8,192;
- source/event age: up to 4.493 seconds;
- WebSocket reconnects: zero.

The decisive interval had a 4.493-second source tail with 1.753 ms parse time,
2 ms event-loop lag, and 3 ms application residence. Increasing our strategy
latency constant or queue capacity would therefore conceal the problem rather
than model it. The correct behavior is to freeze new actions on stale causal
updates while retaining already-resting exposure in scored results.
