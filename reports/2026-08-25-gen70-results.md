# Generation 70 results — transport buffer hypothesis rejected

## Decision

**NO-GO for live money. Keep the new frame telemetry, restore the 1024-frame
limit, retire `basket99x50` and `mintrepair5p95`, and test dust-tolerant
replenishment next.**

Generation 70 produced three valid windows and no reconnect in 20 minutes. It
did not prove that raising the WebSocket internal frame limit fixed anything:
the measured high-water was only 145 frames, far below both the old 1024 limit
and the experimental 4096 limit. The previous 1013 closes remain intermittent
or provider-side. Planned boundary reconnects correctly stayed removed.

The strategy evidence is also sharper. Baseline and x50 headline profits were
mostly favorable settlement of unmatched inventory. Timed completion converted
much of that residual into positive paired economics. The p95 mint repair lost
in both repair-due episodes, while p100 paid away edge for negligible reduction
in completion time.

No live executor or mintbot ran, and no orders were placed.

## Transport evidence

- Three windows resolved with 18/18 valid strategy-windows and zero invalid
  fills.
- Zero unexpected reconnects over about 20.5 minutes and four safe in-place
  token rotations.
- WebSocket internal-frame high-water: 145, from more than 900,000 samples;
  telemetry unavailable count: zero.
- Application event-queue high-water: 146/8192; maximum residence: 6 ms.
- Parse maximum: 1.897 ms; strategy-loop p50/p90: about 1 ms; loop lifetime
  maximum: 25 ms.
- Measured simulated action activation: 68-71 ms.

The old 1024-frame threshold never engaged, so a 4096 limit cannot be credited
for the clean run. Keeping it would be cargo-cult tuning. The next revision
should restore 1024 while retaining telemetry.

Normal event age was commonly 10-15 ms, not one second. The source nevertheless
produced repeated bursts above the 400 ms safety cutoff: 9,032 stale causal
events were rejected and lifetime source-event age reached 2.288 seconds while
local queue residence stayed at most 6 ms. This is upstream/timestamp tail risk,
not local loop latency. All three scored windows were marked lag-exposed, which
prevents treating their PnL as clean strategy evidence.

### Basket interpretation

`basket99x50` was identical to baseline for a third generation. Capital capacity
is not binding; keeping this arm wastes experiment capacity.

Baseline/x50 accumulated 57.8 fill-shares but ended with 12.3 unmatched shares.
Their +$5.21 realized PnL contained +$6.13 of favorable outcome luck. Marking
the residual at 50 cents gives -$0.92, and adverse settlement gives -$7.05.
Calling that profitable would be self-deception.

`basket99t270d` saw the same maker fills, then used bounded late taker completion.
It ended with only 2.3 unmatched shares and produced +$3.97 neutral / +$2.84
adverse-floor PnL. In the 19:05 and 19:15 windows it converted five-share
residuals into complete pairs at total costs below the configured $0.99 cap.
The 19:10 window retained a 2.26-share residual. Three lagged windows are far
too few for promotion, but this is materially better inventory control than
baseline.

### Mint interpretation

The pure maker control completed all three five-set cycles at gross pair
proceeds of $1.03 and modeled +$0.15 per window.

Repair was due in two windows:

- p95 locked -$0.1656 and -$0.2316; combined loss -$0.3972 versus +$0.30 for
  the maker control in the same episodes.
- p100 locked +$0.0419 and +$0.0642; combined +$0.1061 versus +$0.30 for the
  maker control.
- p100 completed only about 0.2 and 0.4 seconds before the maker control in
  those episodes. It paid away about $0.194 for virtually no exposure-time
  reduction.

The current p95 repair is dominated and should be removed. The p100 arm is less
bad but its 60-second trigger/floor combination is not earning its insurance
cost. Retain it only as a control while designing a near-close risk rule; do not
increase mint size.

Maker markout was adverse: mint control averaged about -5.3 cents at one second
and -11.2 cents at five seconds. Positive completed-pair economics can coexist
with toxic individual fills, but this makes latency, cancellation, and residual
risk central. Three $1.03 cycles are not proof of live fillability or profit.

## Signal

Only the 19:10 window had a complete official-TWAP shadow: +0.28 bp, outcome Up.
The other two missed opening samples. This magnitude is below exploratory one-
and two-basis-point thresholds and adds no evidence for a production signal or
strength cutoff. The signal remained shadow-only.

## Next bounded experiment

1. Restore `MARKET_WS_MAX_QUEUE` to 1024 and retain the internal-depth telemetry.
2. Remove the proven-identical x50 arm and dominated p95 mint repair.
3. Add one basket arm that treats at most 0.1 unmatched share as balanced enough
   to begin another two-sided five-share clip. Preserve the fractional lot in
   accounting, the $0.99 rolling pair-cost cap, and the 20-share inventory cap.
4. Keep baseline, timed-completion, mint maker control, and p100 control. Do not
   add more than this one strategy hypothesis.
5. Require at least three complete windows, zero reconnects, and compare paired
   shares, neutral PnL, adverse floor, and maximum residual—not headline PnL.

We are still nowhere near the clean historical winner's thousands of paired
shares per market. The current evidence supports paired maker accumulation with
strict inventory completion; it does not support universal minting, prediction,
or scaling capital.
